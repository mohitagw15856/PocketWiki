title: Taxonomy
---

Taxonomy is the science of naming, describing and classifying living things. It brings order to the vast diversity of life by grouping organisms according to their shared features.

## The system of classification

The modern system of taxonomy was established by [Carl Linnaeus](carl-linnaeus.md), who gave each species a two-part Latin name and arranged living things into a nested set of ranks. This clear scheme allowed naturalists across the world to refer to the same organism without confusion.

Species are grouped into larger categories, so that closely related organisms are placed together and the whole of life is arranged in an orderly structure.

## Connections

Taxonomy underlies both [Botany](botany.md) and [Zoology](zoology.md), providing the framework within which plants and animals are studied. It also proved important for the theory of [Evolution](evolution.md), since the patterns of relationship revealed by classification reflect descent from common ancestors, an idea developed by [Charles Darwin](charles-darwin.md).

The naturalist [Alexander von Humboldt](alexander-von-humboldt.md) relied on taxonomy when describing the many species he encountered on his travels.

Modern taxonomy uses evidence from anatomy, behaviour and [Genetics](genetics.md) to refine the grouping of species. Although the details change as knowledge grows, the goal remains the same: to bring clarity and order to the study of the living world, and to reveal the relationships that connect all living things.
