title: Natural Selection
---

Natural selection is the process by which living things that are better suited to their environment tend to survive and leave more offspring. It is the central mechanism of the theory of [Evolution](evolution.md), set out by [Charles Darwin](charles-darwin.md).

## How it works

Within any population, individuals differ from one another. Some of these differences make an organism more likely to find food, avoid danger or attract a mate. Such individuals are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing their helpful traits to the next generation.

Over many generations this steady sorting causes species to change and become better adapted to their surroundings. The process needs no guiding hand; it follows simply from variation, inheritance and differing rates of survival.

## Evidence and support

Natural selection helps to explain the diversity of life catalogued through [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md) and the relationships between species. The discovery of the rules of inheritance by [Gregor Mendel](gregor-mendel.md) later showed how traits are passed on, giving the theory a firm basis in [Genetics](genetics.md).

Evidence from [Fossils](fossils.md), from the study of [Zoology](zoology.md) and [Botany](botany.md), and from the geographical distribution of species all supports the idea. Natural selection remains one of the most powerful concepts in biology, uniting the whole living world under a single explanatory principle and continuing to guide research today.
