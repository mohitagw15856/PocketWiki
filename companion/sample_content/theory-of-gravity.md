---
title: Theory of Gravity
---

The theory of gravity describes the attraction between objects that have mass. In its classical form it was set out by [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md), who proposed that every body draws every other body towards it.

## Universal gravitation

Newton's law states that the force of attraction between two objects grows with their masses and weakens with the square of the distance between them. This single principle explained both why objects fall to the ground and why the [Planets](planets.md) travel around [The Sun](the-sun.md).

The theory accounted for the elliptical orbits found by [Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md) and united the motion of bodies on Earth with the motion of the heavens. It became a cornerstone of [Physics](physics.md).

## Reach and refinement

Gravity governs the tides, the paths of [Comets](comets.md) and the structure of the whole [Solar System](the-solar-system.md). It shapes the orbit of [The Moon](the-moon.md) around the Earth and holds together vast collections of stars such as the [Milky Way](milky-way.md).

Newton's account was later extended by more modern theories that describe gravity in terms of the curving of space and time, yet his law remains accurate for everyday purposes and for most calculations in [Astronomy](astronomy.md). The theory of gravity stands as one of the great unifying ideas of science, linking the fall of an apple to the motion of the stars.
