---
title: Robert Hooke
---

Robert Hooke (1635 to 1703) was an English scientist of extraordinary range, active in [Physics](physics.md), biology, astronomy and architecture. He served as a leading experimenter for the Royal Society, devising and performing demonstrations across many fields.

## The microscope and Micrographia

Hooke was a master of the microscope. In his book *Micrographia* he published detailed drawings of insects, plants and other small objects. Examining a thin slice of cork, he saw tiny compartments that he named cells, a term still central to [Cell Theory](cell-theory.md). His work paralleled the observations of [Antonie van Leeuwenhoek](antonie-van-leeuwenhoek.md).

## Physics and disputes

Hooke discovered the law of elasticity that bears his name, stating that the extension of a spring is proportional to the force applied. He studied [Optics](optics.md), gravitation and the motion of the [Planets](planets.md), and he took part in famous disputes with [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md) over credit for scientific ideas.

Hooke also helped to rebuild London after the Great Fire, working as a surveyor and architect. His curiosity seemed boundless, and his contributions to [Geology](geology.md) included early thoughts on [Fossils](fossils.md) as the remains of once-living creatures. He is remembered as one of the most versatile and inventive figures of his age.
