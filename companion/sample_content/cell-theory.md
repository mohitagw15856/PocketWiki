---
title: Cell Theory
---

Cell theory is the principle that all living things are made of cells, and that the cell is the basic unit of life. It is one of the foundations of modern biology.

## Main ideas

Cell theory holds three central claims:

1. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
2. The cell is the smallest unit that can be called alive.
3. All cells arise from existing cells.

Together these ideas show that the great variety of life shares a common structure at the smallest scale.

## Discovery

The word cell was introduced by [Robert Hooke](robert-hooke.md), who saw tiny compartments while examining cork under a microscope. The Dutch observer [Antonie van Leeuwenhoek](antonie-van-leeuwenhoek.md) then revealed a hidden world of living cells and microbes.

Later scientists combined many observations to state cell theory in its full form. The theory connects closely to [Genetics](genetics.md), since cells carry the material of inheritance studied by [Gregor Mendel](gregor-mendel.md), and to [Zoology](zoology.md) and [Botany](botany.md), which examine the cells of animals and plants.

Cell theory transformed the study of life, showing that growth, reproduction and disease all involve processes within cells. It remains a guiding idea across biology, uniting the study of the smallest living units with the understanding of whole organisms and the diversity revealed by [Evolution](evolution.md).
