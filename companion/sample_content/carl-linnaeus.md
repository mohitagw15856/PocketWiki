---
title: Carl Linnaeus
---

Carl Linnaeus (1707 to 1778) was a Swedish naturalist who created the modern system of naming and classifying living things. His work forms the basis of [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md) and remains central to both [Botany](botany.md) and [Zoology](zoology.md).

## Binomial naming

Linnaeus introduced a consistent two-part name for each species, giving a genus followed by a specific name. This simple scheme replaced the long and varied descriptions used before, and it allowed scholars across different countries to refer to the same organism without confusion.

He grouped species into a nested hierarchy of ranks, arranging the diversity of life into an orderly structure.

## Influence

The clear system of [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md) that Linnaeus provided later helped [Charles Darwin](charles-darwin.md) to think about the relationships between species and about [Evolution](evolution.md). Naturalists such as [Alexander von Humboldt](alexander-von-humboldt.md) relied on his methods when describing the plants and animals they encountered on their travels.

Linnaeus was a dedicated teacher who sent students across the world to collect specimens. His passion for order shaped the study of natural history for generations. Although later scientists revised many of his groupings, the framework he devised, and above all his consistent naming, endures as one of the foundations of biology.
