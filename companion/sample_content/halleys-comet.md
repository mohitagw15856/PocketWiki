---
title: Halley's Comet
---

Halley's Comet is the most famous of all [Comets](comets.md). It returns to the inner [Solar System](the-solar-system.md) roughly every seventy-six years and can be seen with the naked eye when it passes close to [The Sun](the-sun.md).

## A predictable visitor

The comet is named after the English astronomer Edmond Halley, who studied records of earlier bright comets. He realised that several past sightings were in fact the same object returning again and again. Using the [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md) of [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md), he calculated its orbit and predicted that it would return.

When the comet reappeared as forecast, after Halley's death, it was a striking confirmation of Newton's ideas and of the power of [Astronomy](astronomy.md) to predict events far in the future.

## Significance

Halley's Comet had been observed for many centuries before its nature was understood, and it appears in old records and works of art. Its regular return demonstrated that comets obey the same laws of motion as the [Planets](planets.md), a key insight following the work of [Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md).

Each appearance of the comet is a notable event, connecting one generation of observers to the next. Like other comets, it releases gas and dust as it nears the Sun, producing a bright head and a long tail.

Halley's Comet remains a celebrated example of how careful calculation can reveal the hidden order of the heavens.
