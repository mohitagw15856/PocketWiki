---
title: The Moon
---

The Moon is the Earth's only natural satellite and the brightest object in the night sky after [The Sun](the-sun.md). It orbits the Earth roughly once a month and has been a subject of wonder and study since ancient times.

## Features and motion

The Moon is a rocky world, marked with craters, plains and mountains. It has no air or liquid water on its surface. As it circles the Earth, the changing angle of sunlight causes the familiar cycle of phases, from new moon to full moon and back again.

The Moon is held in its orbit by the [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md) of [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md), and its own gravity is the main cause of the tides in the seas.

## Observation

When [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md) first turned a [Telescope](telescope.md) towards the Moon, he saw mountains and craters, showing that it was a world in its own right rather than a smooth, perfect sphere. This helped to support the ideas of [Heliocentrism](heliocentrism.md).

The Moon is the nearest neighbour of the Earth in the [Solar System](the-solar-system.md) and the only other world that people have visited. Its motion has long been used to mark the passage of time.

The study of the Moon remains an important part of [Astronomy](astronomy.md). It offers clues about the history of the Earth and about the early days of the whole [Solar System](the-solar-system.md).
