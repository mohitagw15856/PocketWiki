The telescope is an instrument that gathers light to reveal distant objects in greater detail. Its invention transformed [Astronomy](astronomy.md) by allowing observers to see far more than the unaided eye can.

## How it works

A telescope collects light using either a lens or a curved mirror and brings it to a focus, forming a magnified image. Instruments that use lenses are called refractors, while those that use mirrors are called reflectors. The design of the telescope rests on the principles of [Optics](optics.md).

## History and impact

Early telescopes used lenses, and [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md) was among the first to turn one towards the sky. With it he discovered the moons of [Jupiter](jupiter.md), the craters of [The Moon](the-moon.md) and the countless stars of the [Milky Way](milky-way.md), findings that supported [Heliocentrism](heliocentrism.md).

Later, [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md) built a reflecting telescope that used a mirror instead of a lens, avoiding some of the colour distortions of early instruments. The understanding of lenses was also advanced by [Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md).

The telescope revealed that the universe is far larger and richer than anyone had imagined, showing the [Planets](planets.md) as worlds and the sky as filled with distant stars. Ever larger and more powerful telescopes have continued this work, and the instrument remains the central tool of astronomy, opening the depths of space to human study.
