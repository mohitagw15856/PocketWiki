---
title: Tycho Brahe
---

Tycho Brahe (1546 to 1601) was a Danish astronomer celebrated for the accuracy and completeness of his observations of the sky. Working before the invention of the [Telescope](telescope.md), he measured the positions of stars and [Planets](planets.md) with remarkable precision using large instruments.

## Observations

From his well-equipped observatory, Tycho recorded the heavens night after night for many years. He observed a bright new star and studied [Comets](comets.md), showing that they travel far beyond the [Moon](the-moon.md). These findings challenged the old belief that the heavens were unchanging.

## A bridge in astronomy

Tycho did not fully accept [Heliocentrism](heliocentrism.md); he proposed a compromise model of his own. Yet the true value of his work lay in his data. After his death his careful records passed to his assistant [Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md), who used them to discover the laws of planetary motion.

In this way Tycho's patient measurement made possible a great advance in [Astronomy](astronomy.md). His example showed that progress in [Physics](physics.md) and astronomy often depends on precise observation as much as on bold theory. Tycho was a colourful figure with a keen sense of the importance of instruments, and his legacy rests on the extraordinary quality of the observations he left behind.
