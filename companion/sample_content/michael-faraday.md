---
title: Michael Faraday
---

Michael Faraday (1791 to 1867) was an English scientist whose experiments established key principles of [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md). Largely self-taught, he rose from humble beginnings to become one of the most influential experimenters in the history of [Physics](physics.md).

## Electromagnetic discoveries

Faraday showed that a changing magnetic field can produce an electric current, a phenomenon known as electromagnetic induction. This discovery underlies the electric generators and transformers that power modern life. He also studied the effect of magnetism on light and introduced the idea of fields of force that fill the space around magnets and charges.

## Chemistry and lectures

Faraday made important contributions to [Chemistry](chemistry.md) as well, including work on the laws that govern electrical decomposition. He was a gifted communicator who delighted audiences with public demonstrations.

His practical genius complemented the more mathematical approach of later thinkers, and his notion of fields influenced the whole of [Optics](optics.md) and [Physics](physics.md). Like [Marie Curie](marie-curie.md), he combined patient experiment with a deep curiosity about the underlying nature of matter.

Faraday declined many honours and preferred a modest life of research. He is remembered as a model of the careful, imaginative experimenter, whose findings connected electricity, magnetism and light in ways that shaped the science that followed.
