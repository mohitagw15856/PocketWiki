---
title: Marie Curie
---

Marie Curie (1867 to 1934) was a physicist and chemist, born in Poland and working chiefly in France, who carried out pioneering research on radioactivity. She was the first person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.

## Discoveries

With her husband Pierre, Curie studied minerals that emitted mysterious rays. Through patient chemical work she isolated two new elements, polonium and radium, adding to the [Periodic Table](periodic-table.md). Her research helped to reveal that atoms are not unchanging, a finding of deep importance to [Physics](physics.md) and [Chemistry](chemistry.md).

## Method and legacy

Curie's work required processing large quantities of ore to extract tiny amounts of material. Her careful measurements and persistence set a standard for experimental science, in the tradition of earlier investigators such as [Michael Faraday](michael-faraday.md).

During the First World War she developed mobile X-ray units to help treat wounded soldiers, showing how discoveries in [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md) and radiation could serve medicine.

Curie faced considerable obstacles as a woman in science, yet she became a professor and a leading figure in her field, much as [Ada Lovelace](ada-lovelace.md) had earlier shown the reach of women in mathematical work. She is remembered for her intellectual courage and for the practical benefits her discoveries brought to the world.
