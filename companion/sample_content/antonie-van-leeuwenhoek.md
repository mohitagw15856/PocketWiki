---
title: Antonie van Leeuwenhoek
---

Antonie van Leeuwenhoek (1632 to 1723) was a Dutch tradesman and self-taught scientist who is often called the father of microbiology. Using microscopes he made himself, he became the first person to observe many kinds of tiny living things.

## The little animals

Leeuwenhoek ground exceptionally fine lenses and built simple microscopes of great power. Through them he observed bacteria, single-celled organisms, blood cells and the structure of muscle. He called the tiny creatures he saw in water his *little animals*, and his reports astonished the scholars of his time.

## Contribution to biology

His discoveries greatly extended the microscopic world first revealed by [Robert Hooke](robert-hooke.md) and provided vital evidence for [Cell Theory](cell-theory.md). By showing that life exists at scales far below the reach of the naked eye, he opened new paths in [Zoology](zoology.md) and [Botany](botany.md).

Leeuwenhoek kept the exact methods of making his best lenses secret, and the quality of his instruments was not matched for many years. Though he had no formal training, his patience and sharp eye made him a careful and reliable observer. His letters to the Royal Society brought his findings to a wide audience, and his work laid a foundation for the later study of [Genetics](genetics.md) and the biology of the cell.
