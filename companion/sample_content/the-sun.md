The Sun is the star at the centre of the [Solar System](the-solar-system.md). It is by far the largest and most massive object in our neighbourhood of space, and its light and heat make life on Earth possible.

## Nature of the Sun

The Sun is an enormous ball of hot gas. Deep in its core, energy is produced that eventually reaches the surface and streams out into space as light and heat. This steady output drives the weather and the seasons and warms the [Planets](planets.md) that orbit it.

The gravity of the Sun holds the whole Solar System together, in keeping with the [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md) of [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md).

## The Sun in astronomy

The recognition that the Sun, not the Earth, lies at the centre of the system was the great achievement of [Heliocentrism](heliocentrism.md), argued by [Nicolaus Copernicus](nicolaus-copernicus.md). Early telescopic study by [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md) revealed dark spots on its surface, showing that the Sun is not perfect and unchanging.

The Sun connects to the study of [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics.md), since it is a vast source of energy, and to [Optics](optics.md), through the analysis of its light. It is one of many billions of stars in the [Milky Way](milky-way.md).

The Sun remains a central object of study in [Astronomy](astronomy.md), and understanding it helps to explain both our own world and the nature of stars throughout the universe.
