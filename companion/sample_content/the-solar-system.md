The Solar System is the family of objects held together by the gravity of [The Sun](the-sun.md). It includes the [Planets](planets.md) and their moons, together with smaller bodies such as [Comets](comets.md) and countless pieces of rock and ice.

## Structure

At the centre lies the Sun, which contains almost all the mass of the system. Around it move the planets, including [Mars](mars.md), [Jupiter](jupiter.md) and [Saturn](saturn.md), each following its own orbit. The inner planets are smaller and rocky, while the outer planets are large and made largely of gas.

The whole system is held together by the [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md) described by [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md).

## Understanding the system

For most of history the Earth was thought to be at the centre. The model of [Heliocentrism](heliocentrism.md), proposed by [Nicolaus Copernicus](nicolaus-copernicus.md), placed the Sun at the centre instead. [Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md) then showed that the planets move in ellipses, and [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md) used the [Telescope](telescope.md) to reveal new details of the heavens.

The Solar System is our immediate neighbourhood in space, itself a small part of the vast [Milky Way](milky-way.md). Its study lies at the heart of [Astronomy](astronomy.md). Through careful observation and measurement, scientists have built a detailed picture of the worlds that surround us and the forces that keep them in motion.
