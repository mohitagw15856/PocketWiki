---
title: Blaise Pascal
---

Blaise Pascal (1623 to 1662) was a French mathematician, physicist and writer who made lasting contributions to several fields. A precocious talent, he produced original work in [Geometry](geometry.md) while still young.

## Mathematics and physics

Pascal helped to found the mathematical theory of probability, working on problems of chance. In [Physics](physics.md) he studied pressure in fluids and the behaviour of the atmosphere, showing that air has weight and that pressure falls with height. The principle that pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted throughout it bears his name.

He also built one of the first mechanical calculating machines, an achievement that connects him to the later work of [Ada Lovelace](ada-lovelace.md) on computation.

## Wider influence

Pascal's investigations touched on ideas later developed in [Calculus](calculus.md), and his study of curves and figures enriched [Geometry](geometry.md). Beyond science he was a thoughtful writer on philosophy and faith.

Like his contemporary [Robert Hooke](robert-hooke.md), Pascal combined practical experiment with theoretical insight. His short life was marked by frequent ill health, yet his output was remarkable in both quality and range. He is remembered as an inventive thinker whose work bridged mathematics, experimental science and reflection on the human condition.
