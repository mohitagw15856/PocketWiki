---
title: Isaac Newton
---

Sir Isaac Newton (1642 to 1727) was an English mathematician, physicist and astronomer whose work laid much of the foundation of classical science. He is best remembered for formulating the [Laws of Motion](laws-of-motion.md) and for describing universal gravitation, set out in his [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md).

## Life and work

Newton studied at the University of Cambridge, where he later held the Lucasian professorship of mathematics. During an outbreak of plague he retired to the countryside, and in that productive period he developed ideas that reshaped natural philosophy.

His major book, often called the *Principia*, presented the mathematical principles of motion and gravitation. He also made lasting contributions to [Optics](optics.md), showing that white light is composed of a spectrum of colours, and he shares credit for the invention of [Calculus](calculus.md).

## Legacy

Newton's methods brought a new rigour to [Physics](physics.md), uniting the motion of falling bodies on Earth with the orbits of the [Planets](planets.md). His influence extended across [Astronomy](astronomy.md) and mathematics for centuries.

Among his practical achievements was the reflecting [Telescope](telescope.md), which used a curved mirror rather than a lens. Newton served as Master of the Royal Mint and was President of the Royal Society. He remains one of the most influential figures in the history of science, admired for both his experimental care and his theoretical reach.
