title: Calculus
---

Calculus is the branch of mathematics that studies change and motion. It provides methods for finding rates of change and for adding up quantities that vary continuously.

## Two central ideas

Calculus rests on two connected ideas. The first, differentiation, measures how quickly one quantity changes with respect to another, such as the speed of a moving object. The second, integration, finds the total accumulated from a changing quantity, such as the distance travelled over time.

These two operations are closely linked, and together they form a powerful tool for solving a wide range of problems.

## Origins and uses

Calculus was developed in the seventeenth century, with [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md) and the German mathematician Gottfried Leibniz arriving at its main ideas independently. Newton used it to express his [Laws of Motion](laws-of-motion.md) and his [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md).

Calculus grew from earlier work in [Geometry](geometry.md), including the study of curves and areas by mathematicians such as [Blaise Pascal](blaise-pascal.md). Today it is essential throughout [Physics](physics.md), engineering and [Astronomy](astronomy.md), and it underlies the mathematical description of nearly every changing system.

The invention of calculus was one of the great achievements in the history of mathematics. It gave scientists a precise language for describing the natural world, and it remains a foundation of quantitative thought across the sciences.
