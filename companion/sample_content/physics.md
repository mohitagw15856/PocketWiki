title: Physics
---

Physics is the science of matter, energy, motion and the fundamental forces of nature. It seeks to describe the basic rules that govern the physical world, from the smallest particles to the largest structures in the universe.

## Foundations

Much of classical physics rests on the work of [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md), whose [Laws of Motion](laws-of-motion.md) and [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md) described how objects move and attract one another. Earlier, [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md) had shown that motion follows regular mathematical patterns.

Physics uses mathematics as its language, drawing especially on [Calculus](calculus.md) and [Geometry](geometry.md) to express its laws with precision.

## Branches

Physics includes many fields. [Optics](optics.md) studies light, [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md) deals with electric and magnetic forces, and [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics.md) examines heat and energy. The work of [Michael Faraday](michael-faraday.md) on electromagnetism and of [Marie Curie](marie-curie.md) on radioactivity greatly extended the reach of the subject.

Physics is closely linked to [Astronomy](astronomy.md), since the same laws govern events on Earth and in the heavens, and to [Chemistry](chemistry.md), which studies matter at the level of atoms.

Physics has given us a deep understanding of nature and has led to countless practical advances. From the motion of the [Planets](planets.md) to the behaviour of light and energy, it provides a unified account of the physical world and remains one of the central pillars of science.
