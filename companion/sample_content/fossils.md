---
title: Fossils
---

Fossils are the preserved remains or traces of living things from the distant past. They provide a direct record of life on Earth over vast stretches of time.

## How fossils form

Most fossils form when the hard parts of an organism, such as bones or shells, are buried in sediment and slowly turn to stone over long ages. Traces such as footprints and burrows can also be preserved. Because this process is slow and rare, fossils offer glimpses of ancient life scattered through the layers of rock.

The study of these layers belongs to [Geology](geology.md), which helps to establish the age and order of fossil finds.

## What fossils reveal

Fossils are central to [Palaeontology](palaeontology.md), the study of ancient life. They show that many kinds of creatures once lived that no longer exist, and they reveal how life has changed over time, providing key evidence for [Evolution](evolution.md).

The English collector [Mary Anning](mary-anning.md) uncovered remarkable fossils of marine reptiles that reshaped the understanding of the ancient world. Even the early microscopist [Robert Hooke](robert-hooke.md) suggested that fossils were the remains of once-living creatures.

Fossils connect the study of [Zoology](zoology.md) and [Botany](botany.md) to the deep history of the planet. By reading the fossil record, scientists reconstruct lost worlds and trace the long story of life, from simple early forms to the rich variety seen today.
