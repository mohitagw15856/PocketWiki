The Milky Way is the vast collection of stars, gas and dust to which [The Sun](the-sun.md) and the whole [Solar System](the-solar-system.md) belong. On a clear, dark night it appears as a faint band of light stretching across the sky.

## A galaxy of stars

The Milky Way is a galaxy, an enormous system containing many billions of stars bound together by gravity. The Sun is just one ordinary star among them, lying far from the centre. The pale band we see in the sky is the combined light of countless distant stars along the plane of the galaxy.

When [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md) first observed the Milky Way through a [Telescope](telescope.md), he found that it was made up of vast numbers of individual stars too faint to be seen separately with the eye.

## Understanding our place

The realisation that the Sun is one star among many in the Milky Way extended the lessons of [Heliocentrism](heliocentrism.md), removing not only the Earth but also the Sun from any special central place.

The great structure of the Milky Way is held together by the same [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md) described by [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md) that governs the [Planets](planets.md).

The study of the Milky Way is a central part of [Astronomy](astronomy.md). It shows that our Solar System is a tiny part of a far greater whole, itself one of many galaxies in the universe.
