---
title: Heliocentrism
---

Heliocentrism is the model of the [Solar System](the-solar-system.md) in which [The Sun](the-sun.md) lies at the centre and the Earth and other [Planets](planets.md) move around it. It replaced the older belief that the Earth stood still at the centre of everything.

## Development of the idea

The modern heliocentric model was proposed by [Nicolaus Copernicus](nicolaus-copernicus.md) in the sixteenth century. He argued that placing the Sun at the centre gave a simpler account of the movements seen in the sky.

The model was greatly strengthened by later work. [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md) used the [Telescope](telescope.md) to observe the phases of Venus and the moons of [Jupiter](jupiter.md), findings that fitted the Sun-centred view. [Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md) showed that the orbits are ellipses rather than perfect circles.

## Significance

Heliocentrism was eventually explained by the [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md) of [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md), which described the forces holding the planets in their paths.

The acceptance of heliocentrism was one of the great changes in the history of [Astronomy](astronomy.md). It removed the Earth from the centre of the universe and encouraged a wider rethinking of humanity's place in nature. The struggle to establish it also showed how observation and mathematics could overturn long-held assumptions in [Physics](physics.md) and cosmology.
