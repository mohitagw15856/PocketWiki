title: Evolution
---

Evolution is the process by which living things change across generations, giving rise over long periods to the great variety of life on Earth. It is one of the central ideas of modern biology.

## The theory

The most influential account of evolution was given by [Charles Darwin](charles-darwin.md), who proposed that species change chiefly through [Natural Selection](natural-selection.md). Individuals better suited to their surroundings tend to survive and reproduce, so that helpful traits spread through a population over time.

Evolution explains why different species share common features and how they are related through descent from earlier forms.

## Evidence

Support for evolution comes from many sources. [Fossils](fossils.md), studied within [Palaeontology](palaeontology.md), reveal forms of life that existed in the past and show gradual change over time. The classification of living things through [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md), begun by [Carl Linnaeus](carl-linnaeus.md), reflects patterns of relationship that evolution accounts for.

The rediscovery of the work of [Gregor Mendel](gregor-mendel.md) provided the science of [Genetics](genetics.md), which explains how variation arises and how traits are inherited. Together, natural selection and genetics form the modern understanding of how life develops.

Evolution ties together [Zoology](zoology.md), [Botany](botany.md) and [Geology](geology.md), showing that the living world has a long and connected history. It remains a cornerstone of the biological sciences and a framework for understanding the diversity of nature.
