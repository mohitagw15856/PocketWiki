---
title: Nicolaus Copernicus
---

Nicolaus Copernicus (1473 to 1543) was a Polish astronomer and mathematician who proposed that [The Sun](the-sun.md), rather than the Earth, lies at the centre of the [Solar System](the-solar-system.md). This idea, known as [Heliocentrism](heliocentrism.md), marked a decisive break with the older Earth-centred view.

## The heliocentric model

In his great work, published in the year of his death, Copernicus argued that the Earth is one of the [Planets](planets.md) and that it turns on its axis while moving around the Sun. This arrangement explained the apparent motions of the sky more simply than the traditional model.

## Reception and influence

Copernicus worked cautiously, aware that his ideas would meet resistance. His model was later refined by [Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md), who showed that the orbits are elliptical, and supported by the telescopic observations of [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md).

The wider acceptance of [Heliocentrism](heliocentrism.md) reshaped [Astronomy](astronomy.md) and encouraged a new confidence that the natural world could be understood through reason and observation. Copernicus himself was a careful scholar with wide interests, including medicine and economics. His central insight, placing the Sun at the heart of the system, is regarded as one of the great turning points in the history of science.
