---
title: Mary Anning
---

Mary Anning (1799 to 1847) was an English fossil collector and early palaeontologist whose discoveries along the coast of southern England helped to transform the understanding of the ancient world. Working with skill and determination, she uncovered the remains of creatures no one had known to exist.

## Discoveries

Anning searched the cliffs near her home for [Fossils](fossils.md), often at some danger from landslides. She found the skeletons of large marine reptiles, including ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs, and the remains of flying reptiles. Her careful excavation and preparation of these specimens set a high standard for the young science of [Palaeontology](palaeontology.md).

## Recognition

Anning's finds provided important evidence in the study of [Geology](geology.md) and contributed to growing debate about extinction and the history of life, questions later central to [Evolution](evolution.md). Scientists visited her to buy specimens and to learn from her knowledge, though as a woman of modest means she received little formal credit during her lifetime.

Her work informed the study of [Zoology](zoology.md) by revealing forms of life quite unlike those of the present day. Today she is honoured as one of the most important early contributors to the study of ancient creatures, and her patient labour along the shore helped to open a window onto deep time.
