Planets are large bodies that orbit a star. In our own [Solar System](the-solar-system.md), the planets move around [The Sun](the-sun.md), each following its own path.

## The planets of the Solar System

The planets fall into two broad groups. The inner planets, including the Earth and [Mars](mars.md), are smaller and made mostly of rock. The outer planets, such as [Jupiter](jupiter.md) and [Saturn](saturn.md), are much larger and composed largely of gas.

Each planet turns on its axis and travels around the Sun, held in place by the [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md) of [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md).

## Understanding planetary motion

For most of history the planets were thought to circle the Earth. The model of [Heliocentrism](heliocentrism.md), proposed by [Nicolaus Copernicus](nicolaus-copernicus.md), placed them around the Sun instead. [Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md) discovered that the planets move in ellipses rather than perfect circles, and he described the mathematical rules of their motion.

The [Telescope](telescope.md) revealed the planets as worlds with their own features, and [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md) observed that some had moons of their own.

The study of the planets is a central part of [Astronomy](astronomy.md). Each is a distinct world, with its own size, surface and atmosphere. By comparing them, scientists learn about the origins of the Solar System and about the conditions that shape planets both near and far.
