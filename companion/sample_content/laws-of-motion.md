---
title: Laws of Motion
---

The laws of motion are three principles, formulated by [Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md), that describe how objects move and respond to forces. They form the foundation of classical mechanics within [Physics](physics.md).

## The three laws

1. An object remains at rest, or moves in a straight line at constant speed, unless a force acts upon it.
2. The acceleration of an object depends on the force applied and on its mass, so that a larger force produces a greater change in motion.
3. For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Together these rules explain the behaviour of everything from a rolling ball to a spacecraft.

## Applications

Newton combined the laws of motion with his [Theory of Gravity](theory-of-gravity.md) to explain the orbits of the [Planets](planets.md) and the path of [The Moon](the-moon.md). The laws built upon earlier studies of falling bodies by [Galileo Galilei](galileo-galilei.md), who had shown that motion follows regular mathematical patterns.

The laws of motion are used throughout engineering and [Astronomy](astronomy.md), and they can be expressed precisely using [Calculus](calculus.md), a mathematical tool Newton helped to develop. Although modern theories refine them at very high speeds and very small scales, the laws remain remarkably accurate for ordinary experience, and they are among the most widely applied ideas in all of science.
