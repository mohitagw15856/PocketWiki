title: Botany

Botany is the scientific study of plants. It covers their structure, growth, reproduction and classification, as well as their role in the natural world.

## Scope of botany

Botanists study plants of every kind, from tiny mosses to towering trees. They examine how plants make their own food from sunlight, how they take up water and nutrients, and how they reproduce through seeds and spores. The study of plant structure at the smallest scale connects botany to [Cell Theory](cell-theory.md).

The classification of plants relies on [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md), the system of naming and grouping established by [Carl Linnaeus](carl-linnaeus.md), whose work brought order to the great variety of plant life.

## Botany and the wider sciences

Botany contributed important evidence to the theory of [Evolution](evolution.md). The experiments of [Gregor Mendel](gregor-mendel.md) on pea plants, which laid the foundations of [Genetics](genetics.md), were a work of botany as well as of heredity.

The explorer [Alexander von Humboldt](alexander-von-humboldt.md) studied plants across the world, recording how they change with climate and altitude and helping to found the study of plant geography.

Botany connects closely to [Zoology](zoology.md), together forming the broad study of living things. It has great practical importance in agriculture, medicine and conservation. From the food we eat to the air we breathe, plants are essential to life, and botany remains a vital and active field of science.
