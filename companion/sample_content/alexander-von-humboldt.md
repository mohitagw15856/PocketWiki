---
title: Alexander von Humboldt
---

Alexander von Humboldt (1769 to 1859) was a German naturalist and explorer whose wide-ranging travels and writings helped to shape the modern study of nature. He is regarded as a founder of physical geography and of the idea that the natural world forms an interconnected whole.

## Exploration

Humboldt journeyed through the Americas for several years, climbing mountains, measuring temperatures and studying rocks, plants and animals. He combined [Botany](botany.md), [Zoology](zoology.md) and [Geology](geology.md) with careful measurement, recording how living things change with altitude and climate.

## A unified view of nature

Humboldt saw the environment as a web of relationships rather than a collection of separate parts. This vision influenced [Charles Darwin](charles-darwin.md), who read Humboldt's accounts with admiration before his own voyage.

Drawing on the [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md) of [Carl Linnaeus](carl-linnaeus.md), Humboldt described countless species and mapped their distribution across the globe. His great work sought to present the whole of the physical world in a single connected picture.

A tireless traveller and writer, Humboldt inspired generations of scientists and explorers. His emphasis on observation, measurement and the links between different natural phenomena anticipated later thinking about ecology and the balance of nature, and his name is attached to many places, plants and animals across the world.
