---
title: Ada Lovelace
---

Ada Lovelace (1815 to 1852) was an English mathematician remembered for her early insight into the possibilities of computing machines. She is often described as one of the first people to grasp what a general-purpose calculating engine might achieve.

## The Analytical Engine

Lovelace worked with the inventor Charles Babbage, who designed a mechanical device called the Analytical Engine. In her notes on the machine she described how it might follow a sequence of instructions to carry out calculations. She is credited with writing what is often regarded as an early example of an algorithm intended for a machine.

## Vision and legacy

Lovelace saw further than many of her contemporaries. She suggested that such a machine might one day handle not only numbers but any information that could be represented in symbols, including music. This imaginative leap connects her to the wider mathematical tradition of [Geometry](geometry.md) and [Calculus](calculus.md).

Her interest in machines that follow rules echoed the earlier calculating device built by [Blaise Pascal](blaise-pascal.md). As a woman in science, she opened a path later widened by figures such as [Marie Curie](marie-curie.md).

Lovelace combined mathematical training with a poetic imagination, and she thought deeply about the meaning of computation. Her writings are celebrated as an early glimpse of the digital age, and her contribution to [Physics](physics.md) and mathematics is honoured to this day.
