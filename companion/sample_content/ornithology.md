Ornithology is the branch of [Zoology](zoology.md) that deals with the study of birds. It examines their structure, behaviour, migration, song and classification.

## Scope of ornithology

Ornithologists study birds in the wild and in collections, recording their habits, their calls and the ways they build nests and raise their young. Many birds travel great distances on regular migrations, and understanding these journeys is an important part of the subject.

The naming and grouping of birds rests on [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md), the system established by [Carl Linnaeus](carl-linnaeus.md), which allows the many thousands of bird species to be identified and compared.

## Birds and wider science

Birds have played an important part in the history of biology. The variety of birds observed by [Charles Darwin](charles-darwin.md) during his travels helped him to develop the theory of [Evolution](evolution.md) and [Natural Selection](natural-selection.md), since differences between related birds showed how species could become adapted to their surroundings.

The study of birds also connects to [Palaeontology](palaeontology.md), because [Fossils](fossils.md) reveal ancient winged creatures and shed light on the origins of birds.

Ornithology combines careful field observation with laboratory study, and it has long attracted both professional scientists and keen amateurs. It contributes to conservation, since birds are sensitive indicators of the health of the environment. As part of the wider study of animals, ornithology continues to reveal the remarkable diversity and behaviour of the world's birds.
